# Ratas II opening: policy and event sources

This register supports the initial Centre–Isamaa–EKRE focus sequence and its
Porto Franco crisis. It uses Estonian government, parliament, court and agency
sources. Sources were checked on 14 October 2026. Later choices that diverge from
these events are authored alternate history for The Fire Rises.

## Structure and timing

The opening contains ten policy focuses followed by **The Marriage Referendum**.
Every focus costs 35 days. All ten policy focuses must be completed before the
referendum focus can start. The policy sequence is:

1. Pass the Voluntary Second-Pillar Reform.
2. Raise Teachers' Minimum Pay to EUR 1,315.
3. Declare the COVID-19 Emergency.
4. Pay the EUR 7 Extraordinary Pension Increase.
5. Fund Töötukassa Wage Compensation.
6. Cut Diesel and Electricity Excise.
7. Start the 23.5 km Eastern Border Section.
8. Open the Kose–Võõbu Highway.
9. Host the Tallinn Three Seas Summit.
10. Budget 1% of GDP for Research.
11. The Marriage Referendum.

These are 35-day policy blocks, not a claim that every law was enacted on the
exact date a gameplay focus completes. Measures already
agreed in 2019 are presented as implementation, funding or administration during
2020. The dates in the source map below remain the historical reference.

Ten uninterrupted 35-day focuses take 350 days. Gregorian arithmetic from
1 January 2020 places the eleventh focus on 16 December 2020, with completion
35 days later on 20 January 2021. HOI4 can apply stored focus progress, so that
arithmetic alone does not guarantee a focus remains active on a particular date.

The implemented start gate is **24 December 2020**. It accounts for TFR's
14-day stored-progress allowance: a 35-day focus can then take as few as
21 additional calendar days. An unbanked focus beginning at this gate finishes
around 28 January; a fully banked focus finishes around 14 January. Both the
12 January investigation and 13 January resignation choice therefore belong
inside the final focus's window. A player completing the first ten focuses
around 16 December can store the eight days spent waiting for the gate, giving
a roughly 27-day final focus and completion around 20 January. Players can delay
progress; if the referendum focus starts after a historical event date, its
storyline catches up when that focus begins. Tests, rather than this source
register, establish the engine's exact order and dates.

| **1. Voluntary second pension pillar** | On **29 January 2020**, parliament passed the reform with **56 votes for and 45 against**, using a confidence procedure. The president challenged the law; the Supreme Court rejected that challenge on **20 October**. Applications to leave the system were to begin in **January 2021**. The early focus represents passing and preparing the reform, not withdrawals during early 2020. | [Riigikogu: passage of the pension reform](https://www.riigikogu.ee/en/pressiteated/riigikogu-passed-act-mandatory-funded-pension-reform/); [Supreme Court: pension reform ruling, 20 October](https://www.riigikohus.ee/en/node/27591) |
| **2. Teacher pay** | The minimum monthly teacher salary increased from **EUR 1,250 in 2019 to EUR 1,315 in 2020**. Treat this as implementing the new year's pay settlement, rather than an unrelated general education overhaul. | [Education Ministry: teacher pay statistics](https://www.hm.ee/en/ministry/statistics-and-analysis) |
| **3. COVID-19 emergency** | The government declared an emergency situation on **12 March 2020**. Public events were prohibited and school operations changed. The accompanying statement described hospital guidance, protective-equipment procurement and healthcare readiness. The focus concerns the first response, not vaccination or the later pandemic waves. | [Government: declaration, 12 March](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-declared-state-emergency-estonia-until-1-may); [Ratas's parliamentary statement, 12 March](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/political-statement-prime-minister-juri-ratas-riigikogu-12-march-2020) |
| **4. Extraordinary pension increase** | From **1 April 2020**, the pension base amount received **EUR 7 after indexation**. The average pension's approximately **EUR 45 total rise** combined indexation with that extra increase. Do not describe the whole EUR 45 as a discretionary government grant. | [State Pension Insurance Act: contemporary consolidation, section 61](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/en/tolge/pdf/505012021008); [Government: first-year policy review, 29 April](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/today-marks-first-year-government-coalition-centre-party-isamaa-and-conservative-peoples-party) |
| **5. Wage compensation** | The initial Töötukassa wage-compensation measure operated during **March–May 2020**. On **21 May**, the government approved a further **EUR 147.9 million** transfer towards its estimated **EUR 250 million** cost. The focus funds a specific employment measure and should carry a fiscal cost. | [Government: wage-compensation financing, 21 May](https://valitsus.ee/en/news/government-directed-additional-1479-million-euros-foundation-labour-market-services-and) |
| **6. Fuel and electricity excise** | Parliament approved the crisis tax package in April. Cuts applied from **1 May 2020**, originally until **30 April 2022**. Diesel excise fell from **EUR 493 to EUR 372 per 1,000 litres**; electricity excise fell from **EUR 4.47 to EUR 1 per MWh**. A gameplay bonus abstracts the relief and should not imply that the lost revenue was free. | [Finance Ministry: approved crisis tax measures](https://www.fin.ee/uudised/koroonakriisiga-seotud-maksumeetmed-said-riigikogu-heakskiidu) |
| **7. Eastern border construction** | Construction of the first **23.5 km** section began in **summer 2020**. This was part of a longer project begun before Ratas II. The focus begins physical infrastructure and surveillance preparation; it does not complete or fortify the whole border in 35 days. | [Interior Ministry: border infrastructure history](https://www.siseministeerium.ee/tegevusvaldkonnad/kindel-sisejulgeolek/piirihaldus) |
| **8. Kose–Võõbu highway** | The **23 km** road section opened on **14 August 2020**. Construction and planning predated this coalition. Describe completing and opening an inherited project on the Tallinn–Tartu route. | [Transport Administration: opening announcement, 14 August](https://www.transpordiamet.ee/uudised/tana-avati-taasiseseisvumisaja-suurima-teeobjekti-esimene-osa-kose-voobu-teeloik) |
| **9. Tallinn Three Seas summit** | Estonia hosted the virtual summit on **19 October 2020**. Its subject was regional transport, energy and digital connectivity. Use the summit date as the focus's reference rather than conflating the government's investment-fund decision with later subscription steps. | [Foreign Ministry: Three Seas history and Tallinn summit](https://www.vm.ee/en/international-relations/regional-cooperation/three-seas-initiative-3si) |
| **10. Research funding** | The government approved its **2021 draft budget on 29 September 2020**, proposing research funding equal to **1% of GDP**. Ratas described projected spending of **EUR 286.4 million** in October. The focus makes a commitment for the following year, not a claim that this level existed throughout 2020. | [Government: 2021 draft budget approval](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-approved-draft-state-budget-2021-and-state-budget-strategy); [Government: research funding explanation, 14 October](https://valitsus.ee/en/news/ratas-called-policy-makers-researchers-and-entrepreneurs-continue-close-cooperation) |
| **11. Marriage referendum bill** | The bill passed its first reading on **14 December 2020**. It proposed a referendum on **18 April 2021** about the definition of marriage. Parliament rejected the resolution on **13 January 2021**, **26 votes for and 49 against**. The historical April referendum was never held. Focus completion resolves the bill and coalition crisis; it must not announce an actual January referendum or an enacted constitutional amendment. | [Riigikogu: first reading, 14 December](https://www.riigikogu.ee/en/sitting-reviews/draft-resolution-to-hold-a-referendum-passed-the-first-reading-in-the-riigikogu/); [Riigikogu: rejection, 13 January](https://www.riigikogu.ee/pressiteated/riigikogus-ei-leidnud-toetust-otsuse-eelnou-rahvahaaletuse-korraldamiseks/) |

## Porto Franco and cabinet succession

On **12 January 2021**, the prosecution publicly reported suspected corruption
connected with Porto Franco. Its announcement described an alleged
party-donation and municipal access-road arrangement involving Hillar Teder,
Mihhail Korb and the Centre Party, alongside alleged bribery concerning
Kersti Kracht and favourable KredEx crisis-aid decisions. The contemporary
event must distinguish suspicions from proven guilt. Ratas was not named as a
suspect in this release. Do not describe him as personally charged or convicted.
See the [Prosecution Service announcement, 12 January](https://www.prokuratuur.ee/uudised/porto-franco-kinnisvaraarendusega-seotud-inimesed-peeti-kinni-kahtlustatuna).

Ratas informed President Kersti Kaljulaid of his resignation on **13 January**.
The [government's notice that morning](https://www.valitsus.ee/uudised/peaminister-teavitas-presidenti-valitsuse-tagasiastumisest-1)
explains that the outgoing government continues until a successor takes office.
He formally announced his decision to the cabinet on **14 January**, as recorded
in the [government's resignation announcement](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-led-juri-ratas-has-resigned-will-continue-its-activities-until-new-government-0).

A prime minister's resignation did not itself require a parliamentary election.
The existing Riigikogu could authorize a new cabinet. Reform and Centre signed
their [governance agreement on 25 January 2021](https://valitsus.ee/en/governance-agreement-2021-2023).
This supports a scripted cabinet handover that preserves the party ledger and
2019 assembly, rather than exposing a general snap-election decision.

The player option for **Ratas to remain prime minister** deliberately diverges
from this history. It must be written as choosing to hold the government
together during an investigation, not suppressing an established conviction or
proving the allegations false. Both choices occur within the referendum focus's
scripted crisis. The player cannot cancel the focus to avoid the decision.

The referendum proposal's historical defeat and the cabinet's resignation are
separate actions. A resignation choice can sink the proposal while the active
focus still runs to its authored conclusion. Any subsequent focus-tree reveal
or replacement must preserve that conclusion and its one-time rewards.

## Use of the sources

The references establish historical facts and subjects. Focus durations,
modifiers, fiscal costs, confidence requirements and alternate outcomes are
gameplay design choices. They are not quantitative claims made by the sources.
Descriptions should paraphrase the facts and identify inherited projects or
future budget commitments where appropriate. No source artwork is copied by
this register.

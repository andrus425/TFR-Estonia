# Kaja Kallas government: policy sources and adaptation

Primary sources checked on 15 October 2026. The route starts with the historical
Reform–Centre cabinet, sworn in on **26 January 2021**, following Ratas's
resignation. The [government's inauguration announcement](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/new-government-led-kaja-kallas-sworn)
identifies the parties and ministers. The [2021–2023 governance agreement](https://valitsus.ee/en/governance-agreement-2021-2023)
provides context; the policy references below establish decisions and enactment,
rather than relying on campaign promises alone.

The continued Reform–Centre coalition through the regular 2023 election is an
alternate-history outcome in this route. Historical Kallas II policies from the
Reform–Isamaa–Social Democratic cabinet formed in July 2022 are not attributed to
Kallas I. In particular, the autumn 2022 universal electricity service, the 2023
family-benefit package and the later Estonian-language education transition law
are not the basis for this cabinet's policy tree.

## COVID and health measures

| Policy subject | Contemporary decision and enacted basis |
|---|---|
| Extend compensation from the second sick day | The government's [10 March 2021 decision](https://valitsus.ee/uudised/valitsus-kiitis-heaks-haiguspaevade-varasemat-huvitamist-pikendava-eelnou) extended the existing temporary arrangement: employers cover days 2–5 and the Health Insurance Fund starts on day 6. The [14 April 2021 parliamentary adoption report](https://www.riigikogu.ee/pressiteated/riigikogu-vottis-vastu-kaheksa-seadust-2/) confirms the supplementary budget and its sick-pay funding. This is an extension under Kallas, not a claim that she originated the January arrangement. |
| Fund hospital emergency costs and testing | The [18 March 2021 supplementary-budget explanation](https://valitsus.ee/en/news/supplementary-budget-will-help-fight-coronavirus-and-restore-human-and-economic-health) assigns EUR 52 million to exceptional COVID-related hospital/emergency-care costs, over EUR 20 million to testing and over EUR 30 million to vaccine purchase and delivery. Parliament adopted the budget on 14 April. |
| Vaccinate frontline workers | The [22 April 2021 government decision](https://valitsus.ee/en/news/government-decided-continue-vaccinating-frontline-workers) resumes vaccination of workers whose jobs expose them to greater infection risk after supply interruptions. It supports a distinct frontline-worker campaign following the opening's general response. |
| Issue and verify digital COVID certificates | The [Social Ministry's implementation account](https://www.sm.ee/covid-19-kriisi-lahendamine-sotsiaalministeeriumis) records patient-portal certificates from 9 June 2021. [TEHIK's 7 September 2021 announcement](https://www.tehik.ee/uudis/covid-toendite-kontrollimine-muutus-lihtsamaks) documents the operational verification application and its update for Estonian requirements. This is an implemented service, not simply an EU proposal. |

Emergency responses should require actual Estonian cases. Recovery may make an
unused emergency intervention unnecessary; bypassing it without its reward
preserves the campaign schedule. A certificate service or vaccination campaign
does not imply instantaneous eradication. Any change between TFR's case tiers is
an authored gameplay abstraction.

## Social, education and recovery policy

| Policy subject | Enacted or approved basis |
|---|---|
| Legislate the EUR 20 extraordinary pension increase | [Act adopted 26 January 2022](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/102022022001) adds EUR 20 to the pension base and national-pension rate from 1 January 2023. The focus should approve and finance the scheduled reform, rather than claim that the historical payment increase happened in early 2021. |
| Exempt the average pension from income tax | [The Income Tax Act amendment adopted on 8 December 2021](https://www.riigiteataja.ee/akt/122122021005.pdf) establishes the pension-age allowance up to the average old-age pension, effective 1 January 2023. This is distinct from increasing the pension itself. |
| Teachers' minimum pay: EUR 1,412 | The [Education Ministry's 2 December 2021 announcement](https://www.hm.ee/uudised/opetajate-miinimumpalk-touseb-2022-aasta-1-jaanuarist-1412-euroni) records the government's regulation for 1 January 2022, increasing the minimum by EUR 97. It is not the later EUR 1,749 rate. |
| Fund school catch-up after remote learning | The [2022 budget explanation, 30 September 2021](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-approved-state-budget-next-year) allocates EUR 12 million for learning gaps. The same source provides EUR 3 million for children's mental-health centres, clinical psychologists' professional training and municipal community-psychologist services. [Parliament's adoption record](https://www.riigikogu.ee/en/pressiteated/the-riigikogu-approved-state-budget-for-2022/) confirms adoption on 8 December 2021. |
| Community mental-health services | The dedicated EUR 3 million allocation above supports a separate community-health reform, rather than another generic stability focus. |
| Adopt the recovery and resilience investment programme | The [17 June 2021 cabinet decision](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-approved-recovery-plan-worth-near-billion-euros) approves a EUR 982.5 million plan. [The European Commission's 2022 country report, Annex 2](https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2022-05/2022-european-semester-country-report-estonia_en.pdf) records Council approval on 29 October 2021 and EUR 969.5 million in grants. Plan approval and investment preparation fit a 35-day focus; completion of the entire multi-year programme does not. |
| Fund enterprise digital investment | The adopted June 2021 plan includes business-process digitisation and automation, digital skills and broadband. Use that approved investment programme as the basis. Do not describe the EUR 58 million grant call opened by minister Kristjan Järvan in August 2022 as a Kallas I launch. |
| Continue rural broadband | The 2022 budget explanation continues access-network support, with an objective of enabling nearly 40,000 rural households to connect by the end of 2023. A focus authorises its contribution and construction programme; it does not connect every household in 35 days. |

## Energy relief and security of supply

The [18 January 2022 government account](https://valitsus.ee/en/news/prime-minister-kallas-state-will-not-make-its-people-deal-energy-crisis-alone)
distinguishes measures already in effect: electricity-network charges reduced by
50%, gas-network charges by 100%, and compensation for 80% of eligible energy
price increases for households up to median income. These were not the later
universal electricity service.

The [25 January 2022 cabinet decision](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-approved-additional-energy-price-support-measures)
adds household price limits for January–March: electricity above EUR 0.12/kWh for
up to 650 kWh monthly consumption, and gas above EUR 65/MWh for up to 2.75 MWh.
It also provides **EUR 50 million of loan guarantees** for businesses with
temporary liquidity difficulties and increases relevant business network-charge
compensation. The implemented tree selects household compensation and network
charges; it has no separate loan-guarantee focus. Prices and eligibility are historical reference points; any mod
effect is an abstract budget measure, not a reproduction of monthly household
bills.

The [supplementary budget adopted on 18 May 2022](https://www.riigikogu.ee/pressiteated/2022-aasta-lisaeelarve/)
provides a stronger basis for longer-term supply focuses: up to **EUR 170 million
for a strategic gas reserve**, with a condition against Russian gas, and up to
**EUR 30 million for LNG capability**. It also includes energy-saving and small
renewable-generation investment. These decisions were made under Reform–Centre.
Approving reserve procurement and LNG connection funding is credible within one
focus; constructing a complete terminal in 35 days is not.

TFR's Saudi-war energy crisis is a separate alternate-history trigger. Applying
the real measures to that shock, selecting their costs and relief strengths, and
allowing preparations before an uncertain onset are game design. The historical
sources do not establish that TFR's events occurred. Relief must operate on the
Estonian component of the crisis without clearing the parent mod's global state.
The 16 October implementation uses TFR's actual `GER_oil_crash1`–`5` ideas.
Household support, strategic gas reserves and LNG access each earn one recovery
stage; combined electricity/gas network relief earns two. The complete branch
removes the spirit. Equivalent Ratas projects use the same five slots, preventing
double relief if policies survive a change of government.

## Security and civil preparedness

The adopted 2022 state budget supports the historical **EUR 750 million defence
allocation**, approximately 2.3% of GDP, described in the [government's budget
explanation](https://www.valitsus.ee/en/news/government-approved-state-budget-next-year).
This can fund actual procurement, allied facilities and readiness without
creating unlimited free units.

For civil protection, the [government's 24 March 2022 decision](https://valitsus.ee/en/news/government-will-allocate-over-half-billion-euros-reinforcement-defence-capability-state-and)
allocates EUR 86 million to broader crisis capability alongside military
reinforcement. The [Rescue Board's 2022 implementation announcement](https://www.rescue.ee/et/uudised/taenavu-taehistatakse-eestis-esimesed-avalikud-varjumiskohad-1377)
confirms public-shelter marking, evacuation and warning-system work under this
allocation. The May supplementary budget cited above confirms the adopted
security/resilience funding. These programmes can respond to TFR's threats; their
historical justification in Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine must not be asserted
as an inevitable event in the mod's divergent world.

## Route framing and schedule

A cabinet-formation root, eighteen substantive policies and an accountability
finale total **twenty 35-day focuses**, or **700 days**. From 26 January 2021 that
permits completion on **27 December 2022**, before the 5 March 2023 regular
election, leaving **68 days**, provided no unrelated interruption consumes that margin.
Prerequisites should permit choices between short branches; ordinary policies
need no historical calendar gates. Energy preparation cannot depend on waiting
for the parent crisis to begin. Pandemic recovery bypasses must avoid locking
the final report.

The implemented policy branches use four COVID subjects, four social/education
subjects, four recovery/local-service subjects, four energy subjects, and two
security subjects from this register. Energy consists of eligible household-bill
compensation, network-charge relief, a strategic gas reserve and LNG capability.
The formation root opens choices between these branches, and every branch
terminal is required for the final report. All costs and effects appear in
localized tooltips.

The formation and final report are authored framing. Rewards, dynamic-modifier
values, expenditures, coalition cohesion and alternate-history sequencing are
implementation choices, not numbers derived from these sources. The route
retains the current parliament and regular election rather than inventing a snap
election during the handover. The marriage-referendum focus is aborted on Ratas's
resignation and receives no completion reward. This reveals Kallas's programme
while its first focus waits for cabinet confirmation. A response delayed beyond
native completion removes only that abandoned final, preserving all earlier
policies and rewards. The next valid election ends this specific Kallas mandate,
including another Reform victory, before the broader domestic tree opens.
